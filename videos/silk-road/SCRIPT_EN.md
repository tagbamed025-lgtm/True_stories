## p1 · THE MAN AT THE TABLE (starts at 0:18)
> A man is sitting in a library… In front of him, an open laptop.
> On the screen, two windows. In one, an interview. In the other… a dashboard.
> Orders. Payments. Numbers scrolling by, in silence. [PAUSE]
> Around him: bookshelves, students, the sound of turning pages.
> Nobody pays attention to him. And that's exactly what he wants. [PAUSE]
> Because this man… runs one of the biggest black markets in the history of the internet.

## p2 · WHO IS THIS MAN
> His name is Ross Ulbricht. Twenty-nine years old. A physics graduate, obsessed with economics.
> In 2011, he launches an invisible website, hidden behind the Tor network: Silk Road.
> On it, you can buy drugs the way you'd buy a book. Delivered by mail. Paid in bitcoin.
> On the site, nobody knows his real name. They only call him… Dread Pirate Roberts. [PAUSE]
> In two years, more than a billion dollars in sales flow through Silk Road.
> And somewhere, an FBI team is hunting for the man behind the mask.

## p3 · THE HUNT: TWO MISTAKES
> The problem is, Silk Road was built to be impossible to find.
> The servers are hidden. The payments, anonymous. The messages, encrypted. [PAUSE]
> So the investigators don't look for a flaw in the code.
> They look for a flaw… in the man.
> **First mistake.** Early 2011, on a forum, a user promotes a brand-new site: Silk Road.
> A few months later, the same username asks for programming help… and leaves an email address. With his real name.
> Years later, a tax investigator, Gary Alford, digs up that old message.
> **Second mistake.** July 2013. At the Canadian border, a package is intercepted. Inside: nine fake IDs. All with the same face.
> The delivery address… leads to an apartment in San Francisco. Agents knock on his door. The face matches. [PAUSE]
> That day, nobody connects it to Silk Road. But the file exists.
> And in September, the two threads finally meet.
> The FBI now has a name. But a name isn't enough.
> To convict him, they need one thing: his laptop… open. [PAUSE]
> Because if he closes it, everything gets encrypted. And all the evidence disappears.

## p4 · THE DIVERSION
> October first, 2013. A Tuesday afternoon.
> Ross Ulbricht is logged in. He's running his site from a table at the Glen Park library.
> Around him… FBI agents. In plain clothes. Waiting for the right moment. [PAUSE]
> He has to be logged in. The laptop has to stay open. And he has to be pulled away from his keyboard. [PAUSE]
> So, behind him… a man and a woman start to argue. They're both FBI agents.
> Ross turns around. One second. Just one.
> And in that second… the agents grab him. The laptop stays open. Unlocked.
> A specialist agent, Thomas Kiernan, keeps it running for hours… so nothing gets encrypted.
> The readers around him stand up. They were never readers. [PAUSE]
> The most wanted man on the dark web has just been arrested… in the science-fiction section.
> On the screen, everything is there. The accounts. The messages. The Silk Road journal.

## p5 · THE TRIAL
> February 2015, New York. Ross Ulbricht is found guilty on all seven counts.
> On May 29, the judge sentences him to two life sentences in prison. No possibility of parole. [PAUSE]
> He is thirty-one.

## p6 · CLOSING
> For more than ten years, he stays behind bars.
> Then, on January 21, 2025, President Donald Trump grants him a full pardon.
> Ross Ulbricht is free. [PAUSE]
> But one question remains…
> An empire built to be invisible… brought down by a single second of distraction.
